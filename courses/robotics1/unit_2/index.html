Project Management for Engineering Teams

Students learn the project management tools that turn a group of individuals into a functioning engineering team: defined roles and accountability, team charters and decision-making protocols, weighted decision matrices, work breakdown structures and Gantt charts, and risk registers with hypothesis-driven iteration. Every tool is applied directly to the MiniBot Mini Challenge — a real design-build-test-iterate project culminating in a round-robin competition.

Class Lessons
Class 6
Roles, Responsibilities & Team Norms
Introduces the four engineering team roles, role-in-action decision scenarios, and the team charter. Teams draft and sign a charter with a decision-making protocol, then generate their first three collector module concept sketches for the MiniBot Mini Challenge.
📋 Activity: Team charter drafting · 3 concept sketches (top + front view)
Open lesson Notebook template Worksheet .docx

Learning objectives

  • Name the four team roles and explain what each person is accountable for
  • Apply role thinking to real scenarios — deciding who owns a decision and why
  • Draft a team charter defining norms, communication, and conflict resolution
  • Understand the Mini Challenge brief and sketch 3 collector module concepts
Novice: List one responsibility per role; guided scenario matching
Intermediate: Full role-in-action analysis; charter with specific protocol
Proficient: Role-conflict principle per scenario; estimated part count per concept

Class 7
Project Planning: Decision Matrix, WBS & Gantt Chart
Teams run a weighted decision matrix to select their collector module concept from Class 6, then break the build into a Work Breakdown Structure and plot it on a Gantt chart spanning Classes 7–8. Closes with a complete, build-ready design specification.
⚖️ Activity: Weighted decision matrix · Full WBS & Gantt · Design specification
Open lesson Notebook template Worksheet .docx

Learning objectives

  • Apply a weighted decision matrix to select the best concept from Class 6
  • Break the concept into a WBS with phases, tasks, owners, and time estimates
  • Plot tasks on a Gantt chart with milestones and identify the critical path
  • Produce a build-ready design specification a Builder can execute without questions
Novice: Fill in pre-set matrix criteria; complete WBS owner column
Intermediate: Two added criteria; charter-referenced override reasoning
Proficient: Weight sensitivity analysis; build-vs-trial time contingency planning

Class 8
Risk Management, Build & Iterate — MiniBot Mini Challenge
Teams build a risk register, then build and attach their collector module, run a Trial 1, make a hypothesis-driven iteration, and run a Trial 2. Closes with a Unit 2 status update, stand-up, and Gantt revision — full build-test-iterate cycle in one class.
🔨 Activity: Risk register · Build & attach · Trial 1 → iterate → Trial 2
Open lesson Notebook template Worksheet .docx

Learning objectives

  • Build a risk register identifying, scoring, and mitigating 8+ risks before they happen
  • Build and attach the collector module; run a first teleop trial against the design spec
  • Complete a hypothesis-driven iteration, then run and compare Trial 2
  • Write a Unit 2 status update, run a stand-up, and update the Gantt to reflect reality
Novice: 5 risks with guided mitigations; single obvious iteration
Intermediate: 8 risks across 4 categories; data-connected hypothesis
Proficient: Preventive + contingency mitigations; two-option iteration trade-off

Unit 2 Capstone Challenge
🤖 MiniBot Mini Challenge — Official Game Manual
Round-robin tournament  ·  90-second matches  ·  Teleop only  ·  Abridged FTC-style rules

Two teams compete head-to-head at a time, each piloting the collector module designed in Class 6, planned and specified in Class 7, and built and iterated in Class 8. Teams score by delivering 4-inch foam cubes and rubber balls from the common area to their scoring depot — earning bonus points for double deliveries and for maintaining continuous control from pickup to delivery. The challenge winner is the team with the most accumulated points across all of its round-robin matches, not the most match wins.

ScoringSingle Delivery 1 pt · Double Delivery 3 pts · Continuous Control Bonus 2 pts (stacks)
Control limitMax 2 game elements at once · Excess Control Violation = 5 pts to opponent
WinningRound robin · highest total accumulated points across all matches wins the challenge
