Part A — Fill in the Blank Answers
WBS, Gantt & Critical Path
1 A Work Breakdown Structure breaks a project into phases, tasks, and subtasks until every piece of work is small enough that one person can own, estimate, and complete it.
Accept "WBS" as an abbreviation. Do not accept "work breakdown" without "structure."
2 A Gantt chart puts each task on a timeline so the team can see what is happening now, what is next, and which tasks are at risk.
3 When Task B cannot start until Task A is finished, the relationship is called a dependency. A chain of these that determines total project duration is called the critical path.
Award 8 pts per blank (16 pts total for Q3). Both must be correct for full credit on each blank — partial credit (4 pts) if one blank correct and the other swapped but still from the bank.
4 Tasks that do not block each other can run parallel — two people working on different subtasks simultaneously.
Accept "in parallel" or "parallel." Do not accept "simultaneously" — that is the definition, not the term.
5 A milestone is a specific, verifiable checkpoint that confirms a phase is truly complete.
Decision Matrix & Design Specification
6 In a weighted decision matrix, a weight is a number assigned to each criterion before scoring begins that reflects its relative importance.
Accept "weighting" but not "score" — a score is applied per concept per criterion; the weight is the importance multiplier set before scoring.
7 The design specification is a written statement of what the module must do, every constraint it must satisfy, and a complete parts list — detailed enough that the Builder can start without asking questions.
Accept "design spec" as an abbreviation. Do not accept "parts list" — that is one component of the spec, not the spec itself.
Part B — Matching Answers (4 pts each, 16 pts total)
Buffer → C
Float → D
Override → B
Constraint → A
Common error: Students frequently confuse Buffer (planned extra time added to estimates) and Float (slack time on non-critical tasks). Buffer is a proactive planning choice. Float is a mathematical property of the schedule — you don't "add" float, you discover it. If a student swaps these two, award 0 pts for each — the distinction is a key learning objective of this lesson.
Part C — Short Answer Rubrics (8 pts each)
9 Matrix override — what makes it valid vs. invalid
Scoring Rubric (8 pts)
4 ptsIdentifies that the Lead Engineer must document specific reasoning in the engineering notebook before the build begins. Both elements required for full points. "She writes down why she prefers B" earns 2 pts (missing the timing requirement). "She tells the team" earns 0 pts (not documented).
4 ptsIdentifies what makes the override invalid: changing weights after seeing scores, overriding without documentation, or overriding based on preference alone without engineering justification. Full credit requires at least one specific invalid scenario, not just "if she doesn't document it" (which is covered in the first half).
Model answer: "For the override to be valid, the Lead Engineer must write in the engineering notebook — before any build work starts — specifically why Concept B's ball-collection advantage outweighs the 13-point matrix gap. This would be invalid if she changed the weights after seeing the scores (that's bias, not engineering), or if she simply said 'I prefer it' without citing a specific engineering reason."
10 Build-day question reveals a spec gap
Scoring Rubric (8 pts)
4 ptsCorrectly names the design specification (or "parts list") as the deliverable that was incomplete. Specifically: the parts list did not specify the fastener length, so the Builder had to ask. Accept "spec" or "design spec." Do not accept "WBS" or "Gantt" — those schedule work, not design details.
4 ptsDescribes a concrete remedy for next time: "the parts list should specify every fastener by name, size, length, and quantity before the build begins" or equivalent. Partial credit (2 pts) for vague remedies ("be more detailed next time") without specifying what detail was missing.
Model answer: "This reveals that our design specification's parts list was incomplete — it listed the bracket type but didn't specify the fastener size. Any question the Builder has to ask during a build is a spec gap. Next time, the parts list should name every part with its full specification: 'M3 × 8mm hex bolt × 12' instead of just 'M3 bolts.'"
