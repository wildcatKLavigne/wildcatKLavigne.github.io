buffer · contingency plan · design specification
All three are real Unit 2 terms from Class 7 — included to test whether students can distinguish Class 7 vocabulary (planning/scheduling) from Class 8 vocabulary (risk/iteration/communication). "Contingency plan" is especially tempting here since it's risk-adjacent but distinct from "mitigation strategy" — a mitigation acts before the risk happens; a contingency plan acts after.

Part A — Fill in the Blank Answers
Risk Management Vocabulary
1 Anything that might prevent a project from succeeding is called a risk. A living document that lists every one, scores it, and assigns a response is called a risk register.
8 pts total: 4 pts per blank.
2 Likelihood measures how likely a problem is to happen, and impact measures how serious it would be. Multiplying gives the priority score.
12 pts total: 4 pts per blank. Order matters — likelihood first, impact second, matching the L × I formula taught in class.
3 A mitigation strategy is a specific action taken before a risk happens to reduce its likelihood or impact.
8 pts. Do not accept "contingency plan" — that is a response AFTER the risk happens, not before.
Build, Test & Iterate
4 A testable prediction connecting test data to a proposed change is called a hypothesis.
5 A deliberate, data-driven change made between trials is called an iteration.
6 A written summary using a traffic-light system that communicates progress, blockers, and next steps is called a status update.

Part C — Short Answer Rubrics (8 pts each)
8 Hypothesis-driven iteration — missing element
Scoring Rubric (8 pts)
4 ptsIdentifies that "make the intake bigger" is a general change with no specific hypothesis connecting it to the actual data pattern (ball-specific failure, not size-related necessarily).
4 ptsProposes a better-formed hypothesis that targets the ball-specific failure pattern — e.g., "the intake shape works for the flat-sided cube but the curved ball surface rolls out before being secured; we should add a lip or curved backstop specifically for balls." Full credit requires the hypothesis to connect the data (cubes succeed, balls fail) to a specific proposed mechanism.
Model answer: "This proposed change has no specific hypothesis — it doesn't explain why a bigger intake would fix a ball-specific problem when cubes already work fine. A better hypothesis: 'cubes have flat sides that catch on the current intake lip, but balls are round and roll back out — we should add a curved backstop or rubber grip strip that catches round objects specifically.' This connects the actual failure pattern (balls only) to a targeted mechanism."
9 Unchanged score still represents learning
Scoring Rubric (8 pts)
4 ptsExplains that an unchanged score means the hypothesis was likely denied — the change made did not address the real cause of the original problem.
4 ptsExplains the value of this: the team now knows their original theory was wrong, which narrows down where the real cause might be for future iterations. Full credit requires connecting this explicitly to "useful data" or "ruling out a cause," not just restating that the hypothesis was wrong.
Model answer: "This team member is incorrect because an unchanged score doesn't mean nothing happened — it means the hypothesis was denied. The team now knows the change they made (and their theory about why it would help) was probably not the actual cause of the original problem. That's useful: it rules out one explanation and tells future iterations where NOT to look, which is real engineering progress even without a score improvement."
